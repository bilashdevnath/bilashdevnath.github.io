About me

Turning complex waste streams into recoverable resources.

My work sits at the intersection of chemical engineering, environmental sustainability, and materials science. I study how hydrothermal chemistry and separation processes can transform agricultural and industrial wastes into useful nutrient products, carbon-rich materials, and recoverable critical resources.

I am especially interested in research that connects laboratory experiments with real-world deployment: process scale-up, economics, life-cycle impacts, product performance, and data-driven process design.

Featured research

Nutrient recovery & slow-release fertilizer from dairy manure

My primary research develops an acid-mediated mild hydrothermal route to mobilize nutrients from dairy manure, recover them through alkaline precipitation, and evaluate the recovered slow-release multi-nutrient matrix (SRMNM).

Published research

From dairy manure to a recoverable multi-nutrient product

The process combines controlled acidification, mild hydrothermal treatment, solid-liquid separation, alkaline precipitation, and product characterization. Release tests compare immediate water solubility with acidic root-zone-like conditions to evaluate nutrient availability.
